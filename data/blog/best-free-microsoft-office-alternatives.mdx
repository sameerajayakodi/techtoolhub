## How I chose

I looked for office suites that are **free to use** (not just a free trial), can **open and save Microsoft Office files**, and are actively maintained in 2026. For each one I checked the official website for platforms, file formats, collaboration, AI features, licence terms and upgrade prices. I haven't tested every suite hands-on for this list, so where a claim comes from the vendor I say so. See [how I review](/how-we-review).

**Who this list is for:** students, freelancers, small businesses and anyone who needs Word, Excel and PowerPoint files without a Microsoft 365 subscription. If your company runs on Outlook and Teams, or you exchange complex Excel models with clients every day, Microsoft 365 is still the safest choice.

ONLYOFFICE is the closest free match to Microsoft Office because Word, Excel and PowerPoint formats are its own formats, not something it converts to. ONLYOFFICE says you can work with DOCX, XLSX and PPTX files "without worrying about broken layouts or missing elements", and it also opens ODT, ODS, ODP and CSV.

The desktop editors include a word processor, spreadsheets, presentations and a PDF editor that can fill and create forms. For teams, the **DocSpace** cloud adds shared rooms and real-time co-editing. Its free Startup plan allows up to 3 admins, unlimited users and guests, 12 rooms and 2 GB of storage. The Business plan costs $20 per admin per month, and only admins pay: ONLYOFFICE's own example is 3 admins for $60 a month with 40 users and guests free.

There's also an optional AI layer (ONLYOFFICE AI Tools) that works with the AI provider you choose. I compared it with Microsoft's suite in detail in [ONLYOFFICE vs Microsoft 365](/blog/onlyoffice-vs-microsoft-365).

It's free with no strings, and its website describes it as a "free and private office suite, no forced AI".

## FAQ

### What is the best free alternative to Microsoft Office?

For most people, **ONLYOFFICE**: its desktop editors are free on Windows, macOS and Linux and use DOCX, XLSX and PPTX as native formats. If you prefer a fully open-source suite with no account, choose **LibreOffice**.

### Is there a free version of Microsoft Office?

Yes. Microsoft 365 for the web gives you Word, Excel, PowerPoint and Outlook in the browser for free with a Microsoft account and 5 GB of OneDrive storage. The desktop apps need a paid plan, such as Microsoft 365 Personal at $99.99 a year.

### Can LibreOffice open Word and Excel files?

Yes. LibreOffice opens and saves Microsoft Office files, although its native format is OpenDocument (ODT, ODS, ODP). Complex Word and Excel files may look slightly different, so check important documents before sending them.

### Which free office suite works on Linux?

ONLYOFFICE, LibreOffice, WPS Office and FreeOffice all have Linux desktop apps. Zoho Writer also has a Linux desktop app, and Google Docs and Microsoft 365 for the web work in any browser.

### Is Google Docs really free?

Yes, for personal use with a Google account, including 15 GB of storage shared with Gmail and Google Photos. Businesses can pay for Google Workspace, from $7 per user per month for Business Starter.
