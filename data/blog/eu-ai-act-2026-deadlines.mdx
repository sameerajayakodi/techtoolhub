If you live or work in Europe and use tools such as ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Mistral, the **EU AI Act** affects you, even if you never build AI yourself. In 2026 the rules changed again: the **Digital Omnibus on AI** pushed back the strictest obligations and added new bans.

This guide explains, in plain English, what already applies, what is coming next and what small businesses, freelancers and everyday users should actually do. Dates are from the adopted regulation and legal analysis listed at the end, checked on **3 November 2026**.

## The EU AI Act timeline at a glance

| Date                | What applies                                                                                                                       |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1 August 2024       | AI Act enters into force                                                                                                           |
| 2 February 2025     | **Banned AI practices** and the **AI literacy** duty apply                                                                         |
| 2 August 2025       | Rules for **general-purpose AI models** (the models behind ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Mistral) apply                                 |
| 27 July 2026        | **Digital Omnibus on AI** (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744) enters into force                                                            |
| 2 August 2026       | **Transparency rules** (Article 50) apply: people must know when they are dealing with AI                                          |
| **2 December 2026** | **New ban on AI "nudifier" and child sexual abuse material generators**; grace period for watermarking existing generative AI ends |
| 2 August 2027       | National AI regulatory sandboxes must be in place                                                                                  |
| **2 December 2027** | **High-risk AI** rules for stand-alone systems (Annex III: hiring, education, credit, etc.)                                        |
| 2 August 2028       | High-risk rules for AI built into regulated products (Annex I: machinery, medical devices, toys, etc.)                             |
| 2 August 2030       | Deadline for certain high-risk systems used by public authorities                                                                  |

## What changed with the 2026 Digital Omnibus?

The Digital Omnibus on AI was adopted as **Regulation (EU) 2026/1744** of 8 July 2026 and has applied since **27 July 2026**. The main changes:

- **High-risk deadlines moved.** Stand-alone high-risk systems now have until **2 December 2027** (originally 2 August 2026), and AI inside regulated products until **2 August 2028**. The dates are now fixed rather than depending on when standards are ready.
- **A new ban.** AI systems that create realistic intimate images, video or audio of an identifiable person **without their explicit consent**, or that generate child sexual abuse material, are prohibited from **2 December 2026**.
- **AI literacy softened.** Organisations must now take measures to _support_ their staff's AI literacy, rather than guarantee a specific level of skill.
- **Lighter paperwork for smaller firms.** SMEs and small mid-caps can provide technical documentation in a simplified form, and fines for small mid-caps are capped at the lower of the percentage or fixed amount.
- **Stronger central supervision.** The EU AI Office now directly supervises AI systems built on a provider's own general-purpose model.

What did **not** change: the **transparency rules started on 2 August 2026** as planned, and the obligations for general-purpose AI model providers stay the same.

## What this means for you

### If you just use AI tools personally

Very little changes day to day. You should increasingly see **clear labels** when you are chatting with an AI or looking at AI-generated or manipulated content (deepfakes). From **2 December 2026**, "nudify" apps that create intimate images of real people without consent are illegal to place on the EU market.

### If your business uses ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Mistral

Most small businesses are **deployers** (users) of general-purpose AI, not providers. Your main duties are:

1. **AI literacy:** take reasonable steps so staff who use AI understand its basics and risks. Short internal guidelines and training count.
2. **Transparency:** if you put an AI chatbot on your website or in customer service, **tell people they are talking to an AI**. If you publish deepfake images, video or audio, **label them as AI-generated**.
3. **Avoid banned uses:** for example, emotion recognition of employees at work or social scoring.
4. **Keep GDPR in mind:** the AI Act sits on top of GDPR. Don't paste customers' personal data into tools without a legal basis and a data-processing agreement.

### If you build AI products

Check whether your use case is **high-risk** (Annex III covers areas such as recruitment, education, credit scoring and access to essential services). If so, you now have until **2 December 2027** to meet requirements such as risk management, data governance, logging, human oversight and conformity assessment. If you offer generative AI, make sure its outputs are **machine-readable as AI-generated** (watermarking) by 2 December 2026 at the latest for systems already on the market.

## Fines

Under the original AI Act, the most serious breaches (banned practices) can be fined up to **€35 million or 7% of worldwide annual turnover**, whichever is higher, with lower caps for other breaches. The Omnibus gives small mid-caps the benefit of the **lower** of the two amounts, and lets the AI Office impose fines on general-purpose AI providers directly.

## FAQ

### Has the EU AI Act been delayed?

Partly. The Digital Omnibus delayed the **high-risk** obligations to 2 December 2027 and 2 August 2028. Bans, AI literacy, general-purpose AI rules and transparency rules are already in force.

### Does the EU AI Act apply to small businesses using ChatGPT?

Yes, but lightly. As a deployer you mainly need to support staff AI literacy, be transparent when people interact with AI or see deepfakes, and avoid banned uses.

### What happens on 2 December 2026?

The ban on AI systems that generate non-consensual intimate content or child sexual abuse material applies, and providers of generative AI already on the market must have AI-content marking in place.

### Does the AI Act replace GDPR?

No. GDPR still applies whenever personal data is processed. The AI Act adds AI-specific rules on top.
