If you have tried to buy RAM, an SSD, a laptop or a games console in 2026, you will have noticed the prices. This is not a normal price swing. Memory prices have risen so far and so fast that analysts have cut their forecasts for how many PCs and phones the world will buy.

This article explains **what happened, why, which products got more expensive and when it might end**, using company statements, analyst reports and news coverage read on **7 November 2026**. Where I could only see a figure second-hand, I say so.

<Callout type="success" title="The short version">
  **Why:** AI data centres are taking a huge share of the world's memory, so less is left for
  phones, PCs and consoles. **How bad:** analysts forecast memory prices for PCs and phones to be up
  about 130% by the end of 2026, adding roughly 17% to PC prices and 13% to phone prices. **How
  long:** Micron's CEO says he sees demand exceeding supply through 2027 and 2028. **The Iran war**
  adds costs but did not start this: the surge was forecast two days before the war began.

## What happened to memory prices

Memory chips (DRAM for RAM, NAND for SSDs) are sold in different ways, so the numbers differ:

- **Contract prices** are what big manufacturers such as Dell, Apple or Samsung pay under agreements.
- **Spot prices** are the open-market price for loose chips.
- **Retail prices** are what you pay in a shop.

The reports I could read say:

- **Q1 2026:** TrendForce forecast on 26 February that conventional DRAM contract prices would rise **90 to 95 percent** from the previous quarter. Samsung reportedly confirmed first-quarter increases above 100 percent (ET News, via TrendForce, 5 March).
- **Q2 2026:** reports differ. Taiwanese press cited by TrendForce expected increases of up to 40 percent. A Tom's Hardware headline says TrendForce predicted DRAM would jump 63 percent that quarter, but I could not open the article, so I cannot confirm the detail.
- **Older DDR4 too:** the DDR4 8Gb chip's contract price reached **$9.30 in December 2025 from $1.35 in March 2025** (DRAMeXchange, via Korea JoongAng Daily, 20 January 2026). So buying older memory is not an escape.
- **Retail:** I could not find a current retail price from a source I trust. Price-tracker sites quote very different figures for a 32GB DDR5 kit, so I do not repeat them. Check PCPartPicker's price history for the kit you want.

### What the analysts forecast for devices

Gartner's 26 February forecast, as reported by several outlets (Gartner's own page blocked automated access when I checked), says:

- PC shipments will fall **10.4 percent** and smartphone shipments **8.4 percent** in 2026.
- Combined DRAM and SSD prices will be up about **130 percent** by the end of 2026.
- That would push average PC prices up about **17 percent** and smartphone prices up about **13 percent** compared with 2025.

Gartner is a forecaster, so these are projections, not measured retail changes.

## Why is this happening?

The short answer is **AI**. Memory makers are giving priority to AI and server customers, which leaves less for consumer devices, and a fast-growing product is high-bandwidth memory (HBM) for AI accelerators.

The evidence I could verify:

- A Samsung executive said HBM will take **almost 30 percent** of industry DRAM wafer capacity in 2027, up from 20 percent this year (Reuters, via The Next Web, 29 September).
- **Micron** reported record results for its fiscal fourth quarter (ended 3 September): revenue of **$54.23 billion**. It guided the next quarter to **$61.5 billion**, plus or minus $1.5 billion (Micron press release, 30 September).
- Micron's CEO said he sees demand exceeding supply in 2027 and 2028 and has no "line of sight to when supply and demand will return to balance" (earnings call, as reported by The Next Web and 24/7 Wall St).
- Roughly **three-quarters** of Micron's 2027 output is already committed to customers, according to those two outlets (one says "about", the other "over" 75 percent).

No source I read splits the price rise into exact shares (AI versus everything else), so be wary of anyone who gives one.

## Is the Iran war to blame?

Not for the memory price surge. TrendForce's first-quarter forecast and Gartner's warning are both dated **26 February**. The war began on **28 February**. The memory squeeze was already under way, and DDR4 had risen sharply through 2025.

The war has added costs of its own, through helium for chip plants, circuit-board materials and freight. We cover that in [Will the Iran War Raise Tech Prices?](/blog/iran-war-tech-prices-2026).

## When will RAM prices come down?

Nobody can promise a date, but the people closest to the market are not expecting quick relief:

- **Micron:** tight through fiscal 2027 and 2028 (see above).
- **Nanya Technology's president:** prices stay high in a "T-shaped" pattern, with tightness potentially easing in the second half of 2028 (Economic Daily News, via TrendForce, 5 March).
- **Gartner:** expects people to keep devices longer, because replacing them costs more.

TrendForce said in March that new memory capacity stays constrained through 2026 and the first half of 2027. A faster fix would probably need AI demand to cool, which is my reading of the evidence, not a forecast anyone has published.

## What should you do if you need to buy?

These are my suggestions, not professional advice:

- **Buy what you need now, not "to be safe".** If prices keep rising, waiting costs more. If they plateau, paying a premium for extra capacity you will not use is wasted money.
- **Check the RAM and storage in the exact model.** Some makers trim memory instead of raising the price (Held Games reported one handheld maker doing this).
- **Consider used or refurbished.** A used phone avoids new-memory prices, but only if you check it properly. See our guide to [second-hand iPhones in Sri Lanka](/blog/best-second-hand-iphone-sri-lanka).
- **Compare sellers and ask for the price in writing**, especially if you are buying in Sri Lanka, where I could not find published RAM or laptop price data.
- **Do not rely on one retail price.** Check a price history before you buy.

## FAQ

### Why is RAM so expensive in 2026?

Because AI data centres are absorbing a large share of the world's memory production, and memory makers are prioritising AI customers. Micron says demand exceeds supply and sees that continuing through 2027 and 2028.

### When will RAM prices go down?

Nobody knows. Micron does not see supply and demand balancing in 2027 or 2028, and a Nanya executive said tightness could potentially ease in the second half of 2028.

### Is DDR4 cheaper than DDR5?

Not safely. The DDR4 8Gb chip's contract price rose from $1.35 in March 2025 to $9.30 in December 2025, so older memory has also become much dearer.

### Will phones and laptops get more expensive?

Gartner forecast average PC prices up about 17 percent and phone prices up about 13 percent in 2026 compared with 2025. Several console makers and Apple have already raised prices.

### Is the Iran war causing the RAM shortage?

No. The shortage was forecast before the war began. The war adds other costs, which we explain in [our article on tech prices and the Iran war](/blog/iran-war-tech-prices-2026).
